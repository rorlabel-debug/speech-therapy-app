How to use it

Say it! Tap 🔊 so she hears the word, then she says it. Tap 🎤 to record her and ▶️ to play it back. Listening to herself helps her notice the difference. You decide if it was right: ⭐ Got it! or 🔁 Try again. "Try again" never loses anything.

Silly or right? Coco says the word either properly ("cat") or the way she says it ("dat"). She tells Coco if it was right or silly. Hearing the difference comes before saying it.

Little and often works best for a 3-year-old: one or two rounds a day (a few minutes), stopping while she's still having fun. Each finished round (5 pictures) wins a sticker. Keep it playful: praise every try, and if she gets frustrated, switch to stickers or stop.

Good to know: at 3, lots of children still say "dat" for "cat" and "yike" for "like". K and G usually settle by about 3½, and L can take until 5 or 6. So there's no rush: hearing the difference (Silly or right?) is the best place to start.

This is practice at home, not a replacement for a speech and language therapist. If you haven't already, your GP or health visitor can refer her, and many NHS areas let parents self-refer.
